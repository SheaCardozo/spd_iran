# Bank Melli, budget, NIOC, and ILO record search

This audit records the online search performed on 26 July 2026 for the four
economic-primary-source families requested in the acquisition queue. Catalog
records and website transcriptions establish holdings and leads; they do not
become claim evidence merely by appearing here. Persian Majles material found
through Mashruteh must be verified against an official scan before use.

## Result at a glance

| Record family | Result | Current acquisition target |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bank Melli | No open crisis-period bulletin or annual-report run found; four contemporary IMF IFS vintages are now archived as the strongest practical proxy | Extract the IFS Iran sheets with revision vintages and add other open issues only when a claim needs them |
| State budget | Much of 1329–31 was authorized through provisional twelfths rather than a normal enacted annual general budget | Build a dated legal-authority ledger, then acquire Treasury cash statements and national closed accounts |
| NIOC | No evidence found for a published 1951–54 annual-report run; a 1954 parliamentary transcription preserves a detailed NIOC-prepared statement | Use the attributed statement cautiously, then check its aggregates against later UN, NIOC, and scholarly series |
| ILO | Complete official report found and archived | Review `SUP-055` at printed-page level and join it to later NIOC evidence |

## Practical online-substitution policy

Iranian institutional originals remain desirable, but they are no longer a
blanket implementation blocker. The working evidence chain is:

1. use an openly retrievable contemporary international transcription with
   visible definitions and report vintage;
2. compare it with an independent contemporary diplomatic, parliamentary, or
   international record;
3. compare both with specialist book-length scholarship;
4. preserve disagreement, range, and confidence in the claim or mechanic;
5. reserve exact figures or month-specific effects when only a single
   interested transcription survives.

This is a source-confidence rule, not permission to launder an unsourced web
transcription into fact. Mashruteh and Iran Archive remain discovery carriers;
their provenance must stay attached and their editorial prose is never claim
evidence.

## Bank Melli

### What is actually online

- Internet Archive exposes 70 complete issues of the IMF's monthly
  *International Financial Statistics* from January 1950 through December
  1955. Four high-value snapshots are archived as
  [`SUP-057`](AVAILABLE_SOURCES.md#sup-057): December 1950, December 1951,
  August 1953, and December 1954. The [August 1953 issue](https://archive.org/details/sim_imf-international-financial-stats_1953-08_6_8)
  includes Iran annual and monthly series for Bank Melli/National Bank and
  other-bank assets, government and private credit, money and deposits,
  foreign assets, trade, prices, AIOC payments, and oil output, followed by
  definitions and an exchange-system chronology. It is an IMF transcription
  and standardization, not the missing bulletin itself.
- [CiNii's serial record](https://ci.nii.ac.jp/ncid/AA00085687) identifies the
  monthly English/French *Bank Melli Iran Bulletin*. IDE-JETRO holds issues
  135–136, 139–144, 146–183, 185–217, and 223–227 beginning in June/July
  1953; Kobe holds issues 142–229 beginning in 1954. These are exact
  reproduction targets, not online copies.
- The [British Library file `IOR/L/PS/12/3397`](https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/040-000558453)
  is digitized through the Qatar Digital Library and contains complete
  bulletins 81 and 84 from 1946 plus partial material from issues 74–83. Its
  catalog description confirms that the bulletin carried foreign-trade,
  exchange-rate, money, discount-rate, cost-of-living, wholesale-price,
  commodity-price, coinage, and company-registration data. It is too early to
  measure the 1951–53 shock.
- The Center for Research Libraries'
  [foreign-bank-publications inventory](https://www.crl.edu/sites/default/files/2025-01/foreign_bank_publications_list.pdf)
  lists Bank Melli balance sheets and English- and Persian-language
  bulletins, but the inventory does not expose the necessary year/issue
  coverage. A reproduction inquiry must ask for a holdings statement first.
- The crisis-period annual reports/accounts remain unresolved. The later
  annual-report serial identity in CiNii (`BB25521070`) does not prove that a
  public report in the same form exists for every requested year.

### Contemporary parliamentary leads

The Seventeenth-Majles debate of 29 Mehr 1331 quotes a Bank Melli balance sheet
dated 31 Mordad 1331: 300 million rials paid capital, 1.3 billion rials in one
reserve, 1 billion in a fixed Issue Department reserve, and
13,180,045,684.20 rials of August transactions. The
[web transcription](https://mashruteh.org/wiki/index.php/%D9%85%D8%B0%D8%A7%DA%A9%D8%B1%D8%A7%D8%AA_%D9%85%D8%AC%D9%84%D8%B3_%D8%B4%D9%88%D8%B1%D8%A7%DB%8C_%D9%85%D9%84%DB%8C_%DB%B2%DB%B9_%D9%85%D9%87%D8%B1_%DB%B1%DB%B3%DB%B3%DB%B1_%D9%86%D8%B4%D8%B3%D8%AA_%DB%B3%DB%B0)
is a locator only. Verify the speech and figures in the corresponding
`SUP-007` official session scan, then identify and acquire the underlying
balance sheet.

### Best next request

For implementation, transcribe and reconcile the selected IFS country sheets
before asking the user to obtain any physical reproduction. If a mechanic
depends on a month not carried in those four snapshots, acquire the relevant
open IFS issue from the same public run. IDE-JETRO, CRL, and LOC reproductions
remain scholarly upgrade paths, not prerequisites for beginning the model.

## State budgets and actual accounts

The search changes the shape of `E5`. The
[Majles budget-law index](https://www.mashruteh.org/wiki/index.php?title=%D9%82%D9%88%D8%A7%D9%86%DB%8C%D9%86_%D8%A8%D9%88%D8%AF%D8%AC%D9%87_%D9%85%D8%B5%D9%88%D8%A8_%D9%85%D8%AC%D9%84%D8%B3_%D8%B4%D9%88%D8%B1%D8%A7%DB%8C_%D9%85%D9%84%DB%8C)
enumerates repeated one-, two-, and three-twelfths for 1329–31. The 5
Ordibehesht 1330 debate explicitly says no comprehensive 1329 general budget
had passed and describes the makeshift basis used for the next provisional
authorization. The 1332 general budget, already archived as `SUP-046`,
retroactively approved several uncovered periods in late 1330 and 1331 under
1329 credits.

The correct reconstruction is therefore not a hunt for three conventional
annual laws that may never have been enacted. It requires:

1. a dated ledger of each provisional twelfth, continuation, supplement,
   emergency appropriation, and retroactive approval;
2. the detailed 1329 schedules and budget-commission reports on which later
   provisional authorizations relied;
3. Treasury cash statements, revenue-collection returns, arrears, borrowing,
   and national closed accounts showing actual execution.

The online laws substantially resolve legal authority, but they do not resolve
actual cash execution. A record titled *tafrigh-e budjeh* must be checked for
scope: the item found online for 1328–30 concerns the Majles institution's own
budget, not the national accounts.

The practical substitute for cash execution is a triangulation of IFS
government-credit and deposit series (`SUP-057`), IBRD's contemporary 1950
baseline (`SUP-040`) and 1957 reconstruction (`SUP-041`), the UN regional
surveys (`SUP-058` and `SUP-047`), and dated FRUS/CIA deficit estimates already
preserved in `SUP-010`. This supports directional and range-based fiscal
mechanics; it does not justify invented line-item Treasury accounts.

The UN Fiscal Division's *Public Finance Information Papers: Iran* (1951),
`ST/ECA/SER.A/4`, remains a high-priority pre-shock bridge. A
[Google Books record](https://books.google.com/books/about/Public_Finance_Information_Papers_Iran.html?id=s7RpP16vo0cC)
exposes catalog/search metadata but no usable ebook. Confirmed physical
holding identifiers are TUFS `0000671745`, University of Tokyo Law
`4101737544`, Tohoku `01603173869`, and Uppsala LIBRIS `8126976`.

## NIOC

### Why “annual reports, 1951–54” is the wrong default request

The 1331 NIOC statute created accounting and inspection obligations, but the
search found no cataloged or digitized annual-report run for 1951–54. The
earliest clearly identified annual report found online is the
[1959 report held by the University of Exeter](https://humanities-collections.exeter.ac.uk/dame/s/ku/item/16532).
Absence from the search does not prove that no earlier report was printed, but
the acquisition queue must not presume a publication run without a catalog or
surviving copy.

More importantly, the 9 Azar 1333 / 30 November 1954 Majles session records a
direct dispute over publication. An opposition deputy alleged that NIOC had
not published a balance sheet during the previous 28 months. Finance Minister
Ali Amini replied that NIOC had balance sheets approved by its High Council,
read a statement prepared by NIOC, and said he had asked Mr. Biat to publish
a three-year operational account. See the
[session transcription](https://mashruteh.org/wiki/index.php?title=%D9%85%D8%B0%D8%A7%DA%A9%D8%B1%D8%A7%D8%AA_%D9%85%D8%AC%D9%84%D8%B3_%D8%B4%D9%88%D8%B1%D8%A7%DB%8C_%D9%85%D9%84%DB%8C_%DB%B9_%D8%A2%D8%B0%D8%B1_%DB%B1%DB%B3%DB%B3%DB%B3_%D9%86%D8%B4%D8%B3%D8%AA_%DB%B7%DB%B0).
This post-coup exchange is partisan and must be checked against the official
Eighteenth-Majles print.

### Data exposed in the 1954 statement

Subject to that verification, the NIOC-prepared statement read in the chamber
contains unusually precise leads:

- 141,887 inventory items checked against the books;
- pre- and post-nationalization theft-loss series;
- pre-expropriation workforce categories and 1332 employee/payroll totals;
- domestic product receipts from 30 Khordad 1330 through the end of 1332 of
  4,857,553,628 rials, with 3,239,097,112 rials in transport and other
  distribution costs;
- export receipts of 467,174 rials in 1330, 11,156,871 in 1331, and
  156,514,000 in 1332, totaling 168,138,045 rials.

These numbers are not yet approved claim data. They identify the internal
tables and accounting statements that should be requested and provide
cross-check values for a recovered original.

### Revised acquisition target

For current implementation, treat the Mashruteh session-70 text as an
explicitly attributed, low-confidence carrier of the NIOC-prepared statement,
not as a certified parliamentary edition. Cross-check its workforce,
distribution, export, inventory, and payroll aggregates against the UN energy
and economic surveys, the 1959 NIOC report, ILO's field baseline, and specialist
scholarship. Do not build a mechanic around a lone exact figure when only the
web transcription supports it.

High Council minutes, approved balance sheets, auditor reports, and the
three-year operational account remain archival upgrades. Their absence no
longer prevents a cautious higher-level representation of the shutdown and
domestic operating economy.

## Contemporary UN substitute series

Three official UN Middle East surveys are archived as
[`SUP-058`](AVAILABLE_SOURCES.md#sup-058), covering 1949/50, 1950/51, and
1951/52. They independently compile production, trade, petroleum, prices,
finance, and development evidence across the onset of the crisis. The 1952–53
successor, `E/2581` / `ST/ECA/25`, is [indexed and text-searchable online](https://www.un.org/en/development/desa/policy/wess/wess_archive/searchable_archive/1953_WESS_MiddleEast.pdf),
but its legacy direct-download endpoint currently returns HTTP 404. Later
report `SUP-047` extends the chain and explicitly republishes Bank Melli data
back through 1952.

The Iran Archive book/article catalog and site search were checked again for
Bank Melli balance sheets and bulletins, NIOC accounts, and budget/closed-
account titles. It yielded political pamphlets and later opposition material,
not these institutional record runs. Iran Archive therefore remains useful
for locating political documents and named publications, but is not the best
carrier for this particular economic evidence.

Internet Archive also exposes cataloged scans of the UN *Yearbook of
International Trade Statistics*, including records associated with the 1950s,
but the inspected copies are in its lending/print-disabled collection rather
than openly downloadable. They remain an online access lead, not an archived
source. The same catalog search returned no *Bank Melli Iran Bulletin* issue.

## ILO

The complete official PDF of the International Labour Office's
[*Labour Conditions in the Oil Industry in Iran*](https://webapps.ilo.org/public/libdoc/ilo/ILO-SR/ILO-SR_NS24_engl.pdf)
was found and archived as [`SUP-055`](AVAILABLE_SOURCES.md#sup-055). It is the
best immediately accessible institutional baseline for oil-worker wages,
hours, safety, housing, health, welfare, and industrial relations before
nationalization.

Its date is also its main limit: the mission visited in January–February 1950,
before nationalization and the export shutdown. Its workforce and welfare
evidence should be joined to later NIOC records, local evidence from Abadan,
and scholarship rather than projected directly into 1951–53.

## Search conclusion

The online search fully resolves `E18`, partially resolves `E16` and `E17`,
materially improves the working path around `E1`, and refines `E5` and `E14`.
The original Bank Melli run, actual Treasury accounts, and original NIOC
accounts remain unavailable, so they still block false precision. They do not
block implementation of sourced directional effects, bounded ranges, and
explicitly lower-confidence events built from the IMF–UN–IBRD–ILO–
parliamentary–scholarly evidence chain.
