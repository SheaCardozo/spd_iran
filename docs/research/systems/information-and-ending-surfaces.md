# Information, status, and ending surfaces — system claim record

## Documented foundation

The title, monthly briefings, status panels, Research Library, and ending
summarize claims already controlled by the adjacent event and people records.
They add no independent historical seat chart, nationwide vote estimate,
numerical faction measure, or private intention.

The key dated claims are:

- the Sixteenth Majles and First Senate opened in February 1950;
- the Majles approved 98 credentials in session 2, while the Front's
  Kashan/Natanz and Tehran credentials were approved in later May and June
  decisions;
- the oil committee rejected the supplemental agreement on 25 November 1950
  and the Majles approved that rejection on 11 January 1951; and
- the nationalization principle passed through the Majles and Senate by 20
  March 1951, without yet settling the later nine-article implementation law.

## Mechanical abstraction

Qualitative status labels and the four ending scores are campaign evaluations,
not historical measurements. Chamber totals are derived from mutable scenario
records kept separate from historical evidence. The four ending labels
interpret the player's organizational position at a fixed historical endpoint;
they do not alter nationalization's passage.

Deck order uses Dendry's normal random draw stream. Random order is a
presentation mechanic and never changes a documented date, fact, person,
source confidence, or parliamentary act.
