# Research bibliography and source trust registry

This is the running registry for sources actually used by the game and for
high-priority works guiding future research. An entry is not an endorsement of
every conclusion in a work. Trust notes explain why the source is useful and
where it requires comparison.

The complete review of the design bibliography is in
[`SOURCE_AUDIT.md`](SOURCE_AUDIT.md). Respected works whose relevant text is not
presently accessible are tracked in
[`UNAVAILABLE_SOURCES.md`](UNAVAILABLE_SOURCES.md). A source being **Core**
does not authorize a historical claim until the relevant text has been read
and a precise locator recorded beside that claim.

Files actually held by the project are cataloged in
[`AVAILABLE_SOURCES.md`](AVAILABLE_SOURCES.md). Thus the bibliography explains
why a source is trusted and how it is used, while the available/unavailable
catalogs state whether the evidence itself can currently be inspected.

## Status key

- **Used** — supports an implemented claim or representation.
- **Core** — expertise and scholarly standing are approved for a principal
  foundation; access and claim-level locators are still required.
- **Supplement** — approved for a focused question or interpretive comparison,
  normally alongside book-length scholarship or primary evidence.
- **Candidate** — relevant, but access, edition, expertise, or fit still needs
  review.

## Used sources

<a id="sup-001"></a>
### SUP-001 — Mari Nukii, “Protest Events in the Tehran Bazaar during the Oil Nationalization Movement of Iran”

- **Status:** Used.
- **Archive:** [`SUP-001` available-source record](AVAILABLE_SOURCES.md#sup-001);
  local supplemental PDF and metadata under
  `docs/research/sources/supplemental/`.
- **Publication:** *Annals of Japan Association for Middle East Studies* 28,
  no. 1 (2012), pp. 1–34.
- **Stable identifier:** <https://doi.org/10.24498/ajames.28.1_1>
- **Access:** Full article reviewed; p. 10 is the current claim locator.
- **Author expertise and standing:** Nukii is a researcher of modern Iranian
  politics and the Tehran bazaar. This peer-reviewed specialist article uses a
  systematic event dataset constructed from the Persian newspapers
  *Eṭṭelāʿāt* and *Bākhtar-e Emrūz* and engages the major scholarship on bazaar
  politics.
- **Evidence and languages:** Persian press material and English-language
  scholarship; the article is in English.
- **Trust and limitations:** Strong for its narrow subject—organization and
  protest in the Tehran bazaar—and useful for identifying the National Front's
  formation. It is an article rather than the preferred book-length political
  foundation, its principal dataset begins in 1951, and its one-paragraph
  summary of the October 1949 protest is therefore paired with Azimi, pp.
  207–08. Contemporary reporting remains necessary before the opening gains an
  exact date or participant count.
- **Project use:** October 1949 palace-protest opening, p. 10, alongside
  `MAJ-S1`.

### SUP-002 — Mohammad Mossadegh, *Khaterat va Ta'alomat-e Doktor Mohammad Mossadegh*

- **Status:** Acquired primary source; not yet used.

- **Persian title:** خاطرات و تألمات دکتر محمد مصدق.
- **Editorial history:** The established edition was prepared by bibliographer

### SUP-003 — Masoud Kuhestani-Nejad, ed., *Hezb-e Iran: Majmu'e-i az Asnad va Bayaniyeha, 1323–1332*

- **Status:** Acquired edited primary collection; not yet used.
- **Access:** [`SUP-003` available-source record](AVAILABLE_SOURCES.md#sup-003).
- **Persian title:** حزب ایران: مجموعه‌ای از اسناد و بیانیه‌ها، ۱۳۲۳–۱۳۳۲.
- **Publication:** Tehran: Mo'asseseh-ye Nashr va Pazhuhesh-e Shirazeh, 1379

### SUP-004 — Homa Katouzian and Amir Pishdad, eds., *Namehha-ye Khalil Maleki*

- **Status:** Acquired edited primary collection; not yet used.
- **Access:** [`SUP-004` available-source record](AVAILABLE_SOURCES.md#sup-004).
- **Persian title:** نامه‌های خلیل ملکی.
- **Publication:** Tehran: Nashr-e Markaz, 1381 SH / 2002–03.

<a id="sup-011"></a>
### SUP-011 / S20 — Cosroe Chaqueri, ed., *Historical Documents of the Iranian Left*

- **Status:** Partial corpus acquired.
- **Access:** [`SUP-011` available-source record](AVAILABLE_SOURCES.md#sup-011).
- **Editor expertise and standing:** Chaqueri was a historian of Iranian
  socialism and communism whose long-running documentary project assembled
  otherwise dispersed multilingual movement records. The collection is
  valuable because of that archival labor, not because its selection or

  documents and verify important wording against page images or another
  edition.

<a id="s1"></a>
### S1 / MAJ-S1 — Fakhreddin Azimi, *Iran: The Crisis of Democracy, 1941–1953*

- **Status:** Used; Core.
- **Access:** [`MAJ-S1` available-source record](AVAILABLE_SOURCES.md#maj-s1).
- **Local edition:** New York: St. Martin's Press, 1989; searchable OCR scan
  with original print pagination.
- **Author expertise and standing:** Azimi is a historian specializing in
  modern Iranian political and constitutional history, a professor of history
  at the University of Connecticut, and an Oxford-trained historian whose work
  includes extensive scholarship in Persian. His university profile records
  specialist work on Iranian elections and British influence as well as awards
  from the International Society for Iranian Studies:
  <https://history.uconn.edu/person/fakhreddin-azimi/>. This monograph is a
  major scholarly study of parties, cabinets, elections, the court, parliament,
  and the erosion of Iran's democratic opening.
- **Trust and limitations:** Preferred political baseline. Its arguments and
  factual reconstruction should still be compared with Iranian primary
  materials and later specialist research.
- **Project use:** National Front formation and the October 1949 election
  protest, pp. 207–08. Planned uses also include elections, cabinets, Majles,
  constitutional conflict, and coalition politics.

<a id="s2"></a>
### S2 / MAJ-S2 — Ervand Abrahamian, *Iran Between Two Revolutions*

- **Status:** Core; precise locators still required.
- **Access:** [`MAJ-S2` available-source record](AVAILABLE_SOURCES.md#maj-s2).
- **Publication:** Princeton: Princeton University Press, 1982.
- **Author expertise and standing:** Abrahamian is a leading historian of
  modern Iran and professor emeritus at Baruch College and the CUNY Graduate
  Center. His institutional profile lists a sustained body of university-press
  books on modern Iranian political and social history and his election to the
  American Academy of Arts and Sciences:
  <https://weissman.baruch.cuny.edu/weissman-academic-departments/the-department-of-history/department-of-history-faculty/ervand-abrahamian/>.
  His work draws on Persian-language political publications and extensive party
  and social-history research.
- **Trust and limitations:** Foundational for social structure, parties, labor,
  and the Tudeh Party. Some categories and conclusions should be checked
  against later specialist work. The source EPUB provides stable
  chapter/section structure, while its converted PDF does not provide
  trustworthy print-page locators. Prefer the EPUB for searching and reading;
  obtain a print-layout copy when a claim needs a printed page number.
- **Planned use:** Party organization, labor, constituencies, social networks,
  and Tudeh.

<a id="s3"></a>
### S3 / MAJ-S3 — Ervand Abrahamian, *Oil Crisis in Iran: From Nationalism to Coup d’État*

- **Status:** Core; precise locators still required.
- **Access:** [`MAJ-S3` available-source record](AVAILABLE_SOURCES.md#maj-s3).

- **Author expertise and standing:** A recent specialist monograph by a leading
  historian of modern Iran, centered on sovereignty, control, and the political
  economy of the oil dispute.
- **Trust and limitations:** Essential for avoiding a simplistic compensation
  or “50/50” model. Its interpretive emphasis should be compared with other
  specialists and the diplomatic record. The source EPUB provides stable
  chapter/section structure, while its converted PDF does not provide
  trustworthy print-page locators. Prefer the EPUB for searching and reading;
  obtain a publisher-layout copy when a claim needs a printed page number.
- **Planned use:** Oil proposals, AIOC control, sanctions, negotiations, and
  the political economy of nationalization.

<a id="s4"></a>
### S4 / MAJ-S4 — Mark J. Gasiorowski and Malcolm Byrne, eds., *Mohammad Mosaddeq and the 1953 Coup in Iran*

- **Status:** Core; chapter-level author and locator records still required.
- **Access:** [`MAJ-S4` available-source record](AVAILABLE_SOURCES.md#maj-s4).
- **Publication:** Syracuse: Syracuse University Press, 2004.
- **Editor expertise and standing:** Gasiorowski is a long-standing specialist
  on the 1953 coup and U.S.–Iran relations and a professor of political science
  at Tulane University:
  <https://news.tulane.edu/experts/profile/mark-gasiorowski>. Byrne is a

  attributing all claims to the editors.

  and economics.

<a id="s5"></a>
### S5 / MAJ-S5 — Ali Rahnema, *Behind the 1953 Coup in Iran*

- **Status:** Core; precise locators still required.
- **Access:** [`MAJ-S5` available-source record](AVAILABLE_SOURCES.md#maj-s5).
- **Publication:** Cambridge: Cambridge University Press, 2015. The local
  copyright page states “First published 2015”; earlier project references to
  2014 followed cataloging data and have been corrected.
- **Author expertise and standing:** Rahnema is a scholar of modern Iranian
  political, economic, and intellectual history and professor emeritus at the
  American University of Paris. His institutional profile emphasizes late
  modern Iranian history and the interaction of politics, economics, and
  religion: <https://graduate.aup.edu/ali-rahnema>. This specialist
  reconstruction uses Iranian, British, and American evidence to examine the
  actors and sequence of 15–19 August.
- **Trust and limitations:** A principal source for the daily coup chronology;
  disputed assignments of motive or coordination must still be compared with
  primary records and other specialists.
- **Planned use:** August 1953 crisis sequence and coup network.

<a id="s6"></a>
### S6 / MAJ-S6 — Homa Katouzian, *Musaddiq and the Struggle for Power in Iran*

- **Status:** Core interpretive comparison; precise locators still required.
- **Access:** [`MAJ-S6` available-source record](AVAILABLE_SOURCES.md#maj-s6).
- **Local edition:** London: I.B. Tauris, revised paperback edition 1999,
  reprinted 2009; first published 1990.
- **Author expertise and standing:** Katouzian is an economist, historian, and
  long-standing scholar of Iran associated with Oxford's Iranian Studies
  program. His publisher profile records a substantial body of scholarship on
  Iranian political economy, literature, and state–society relations:
  <https://www.bloomsbury.com/ca/author/homa-katouzian/>.
- **Trust and limitations:** An influential specialist interpretation of
  Mossadegh and structural political instability. Compare its wider
  state–society theory with Azimi's institutional history and more recent
  archival work rather than treating the theory as settled fact. The local
  scan preserves print pages, and a separate OCR derivative supports
  discovery. Citations and quotations must still be verified against the
  original page images.
- **Planned use:** Mossadegh, constitutional behavior, political leadership,
  structural fragility, and corroboration of the 1949 opening.

<a id="s7"></a>
### S7 / MAJ-S7 — Darioush Bayandor, *Iran and the CIA: The Fall of Mosaddeq Revisited*

- **Status:** Core counterpoint; precise locators still required.
- **Access:** [`MAJ-S7` available-source record](AVAILABLE_SOURCES.md#maj-s7).
- **Publication:** Basingstoke: Palgrave Macmillan, 2010.
- **Author expertise and standing:** Bayandor is an Iran analyst, historian,
  former diplomat, and former United Nations official. The scholarly publisher
  presents the book as a source-based reassessment:
  <https://link.springer.com/book/10.1057/9780230277304>.
- **Trust and limitations:** A serious revisionist challenge emphasizing
  Iranian domestic agency and questioning how decisively the CIA operation
  explains 19 August. It belongs in the comparison set, not as the neutral
  default; test its claims against declassified operational records and other
  specialist histories.
- **Planned use:** Explicitly modeling historiographical disagreement about
  domestic mobilization, foreign operations, and causation during the coup.
