# U.S. Consulate Tabriz reporting, 1951–1953

- **Search date:** 2026-07-28
- **Scope:** Publicly accessible reports from or explicitly attributed to the
  U.S. Consulate in Tabriz during the oil-nationalization crisis, plus the
  archival route to their underlying dispatches.
- **Result:** Five dated observations survive in accessible official
  publications. Two CIA summaries have been archived locally; three further
  references are in the already archived official FRUS volume. No original
  Tabriz dispatch was found online.

## Accessible reporting

| Date observed or reported | Surviving record and precise locator | What it safely establishes | Limit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 6 July 1951 | CIA, *Daily Digest*, 11 July 1951, section 2 (Eastern), “IRAN. Communists Demonstrate in Tabriz,” [`SUP-076`](AVAILABLE_SOURCES.md#sup-076) | The digest attributed to the Tabriz consulate a roughly 2,000-person Partisans of Peace demonstration organized by four arrivals from Tehran, with speakers connecting Korea to the suppressed 1946 Azerbaijan government | CIA summary rather than the dispatch; crowd size, organizer identity, composition, and interpretation are unverified |
| 5 August 1952 | FRUS second ed., document 114, Embassy telegram 636, 12 August 1952, printed pp. 315–16 / PDF pp. 352–53, archival file `RG 59, 788.00/8-1252`, [`SUP-010`](AVAILABLE_SOURCES.md#sup-010) | The Meshed and Tabriz consuls reported small Tudeh Constitution Day meetings that security forces easily suppressed | The published telegram combines posts and gives no separate Tabriz crowd, participants, arrests, or original message |
| Late January 1953 | CIA, *Current Intelligence Bulletin*, 18 February 1953, item 5, scan p. 5, [`SUP-077`](AVAILABLE_SOURCES.md#sup-077) | The bulletin says the Tabriz consulate had reported widespread arrests of local military personnel suspected of Tudeh activity | One-sentence CIA summary; no names, count, evidentiary basis, or outcome |
| 7 April 1953 | FRUS second ed., document 233, Embassy despatch 337, 1 July 1953, printed p. 620 / PDF p. 657, archival file `RG 59, 788.13/7-153`, [`SUP-010`](AVAILABLE_SOURCES.md#sup-010) | The consul reported more publicly expressed opposition to Mossadegh and greater prestige for the Shah in the preceding two months | An impression based on unspecified contacts, reproduced inside a strongly evaluative Embassy synthesis; not polling |
| 19 June 1953 | FRUS second ed., document 233, printed p. 621 / PDF p. 658 | The consul estimated about 400 Mossadegh supporters at a rally; pro-Shah spectators threw stones and police dispersed it | One rally is not provincial support measurement; the estimate, identities, and causal interpretation need local corroboration |

The April and June observations appear within despatch 337, “Popularity and
Prestige of Prime Minister Mohammed Mosadeq.” The Embassy itself begins by
stating that it lacked scientific polling. Its provincial reports are
therefore evidence of named officials' observations and contact networks, not
a direct measurement of Azerbaijani opinion.

## Archival route to the originals

The published FRUS source essay says its editors relied heavily on Department
of State Central Files in Record Group 59—especially `788.00`, `788.11`,
`788.13`, `788.21`, and `788.5`—and on Record Group 84 Tehran Embassy post
files. It specifically notes that post files are valuable because they were
not culled as heavily as central files.

The current National Archives catalog does not expose a separate
1951–1953 Tabriz-consulate series or digital scans. The most plausible
duplicate and enclosure locations are:

- **RG 59, Central Files 1950–1954:** begin with `788.00/8-1252` and
  `788.13/7-153`, then ask whether their enclosures or cross-references retain
  the constituent Tabriz messages.
- **RG 84, Tehran Embassy, Classified General Records**, NARA series
  `2124150`: political files `350`–`360.2` are in current boxes 29–31 for
  1950–52; political files `350`–`362` are in current boxes 53–57 for
  1953–55.
- **RG 84, Tehran Embassy, General Records**, NARA series `1715291`:
  files `350`–`370` are in current boxes 129–130 for 1950–52; files
  `320.12`–`370.32` are in current boxes 136–138 for 1953–55.

The box ranges are candidates, not proof that a particular message survives.
The appropriate request is for NARA staff to identify the originating Tabriz
message, any enclosure, and any retained post copy for each dated reference.
The College Park textual reference address listed in both series is
`Archives2reference@nara.gov`.

The NARA catalog's separately named Tabriz-consulate series for these years
were not found. A search result for a Tabriz general-record series ending in
1949 does not cover this project period and must not be cited as if it did.

## Research value and priority

These fragments add real but narrow evidence: visible communist-front
activity in 1951, limited Tudeh activity in August 1952, a January 1953
military-security alarm, and two snapshots of increasingly open pro-Shah
activity in spring 1953. Together they show that provincial politics did not
simply mirror Tehran.

They do **not** supply a stable provincial faction map, an ethnic voting bloc,
a measurement of Azerbaijani opinion, or a detailed Soviet/Tudeh command
chain. The originals are therefore an optional targeted acquisition if the
game later implements detailed Tabriz scenes. They are not a blocker for a
national campaign or for treating Azerbaijan as a region with distinct
historical memory and political pressures.
