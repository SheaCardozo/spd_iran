## Makki's oil-dispossession volumes

### Best available substitute stack

1. **Independent Iranian participant account:** [`SUP-081`](AVAILABLE_SOURCES.md#sup-081)
   contains Bazargan's contemporary Abadan and Masjed Soleyman speeches at
   printed pp. 33–39, a contemporary press dossier at pp. 41–69, and the
   detailed later interview *Khalʿ-e yad, sepīdīhā va siyāhīhā* at
   pp. 399–432, with photographs and documents following it.
2. **Fuller participant memoir:** [`SUP-082`](AVAILABLE_SOURCES.md#sup-082),
   chapter seven, printed pp. 267–386, gives Bazargan's remembered
   operational sequence. The dispossession section begins at p. 273.
3. **Contemporary controlling texts:** `SUP-006` contains the official
   nine-article implementation law; `SUP-007` preserves the official Majles
   session scans; and `SUP-059` preserves the Special Oil Commission minutes.
4. **Official administrative evidence:** `SUP-062` and `SUP-066`–`SUP-069`
   preserve filed cabinet extracts, their authentication chain, a surviving
   regulation, and one oil-expense approval. These do not constitute a
   continuous cabinet archive.
5. **Critical synthesis:** the locally archived major works by Movahed, Elm,
   Brew, Abrahamian, and others remain the authority for reconciling
   participant accounts and British, U.S., and Iranian documentation.

The online Sixteenth-Majles transcriptions for sessions 164 (13 Tir 1330) and
180 (20 Mordad 1330) identify especially useful contemporary reports by
Ardalan and Makki. The transcriptions are discovery aids; any implemented
claim must be checked against the official scans in `SUP-007`.

### Non-equivalence

Bazargan and Makki occupied the same mission but later disagreed. Bazargan is
therefore valuable independent evidence for events he witnessed, not a proxy
for Makki's motives or later narrative. `SUP-081` explicitly reproduces the
Masjed Soleyman speech from Makki part I; that passage is access through Makki,
not corroboration of Makki.

No complete non-Ketabnak carrier for either Makki volume was found. The two
volumes remain identifiable through the [CiNii catalog
record](https://ci.nii.ac.jp/ncid/BB08068450), but the only located digital
copies still lead to Ketabnak's controlled reader.

The local *Ettela'at* run already contains 10 Mordad, one of the most useful
dates for Kashani's public position and reports of conflict at his home. The
selected *Shahed* run supplies Kashani-aligned campaign-week publication from

acquired as individual issues when a cited statement requires them; this is a

No complete public mirror of volume III was found. The HathiTrust
[catalog](https://catalog.hathitrust.org/Record/000627569) confirms digitized

be treated as optional rather than active campaign blockers.

## Missing pp. 425–440 in Makki's coup book

Visual comparison of the contents and adjacent pages bounds the missing
interval. It begins during a letter by Labor Minister Dr. Alami to the prime
minister and includes items headed:

- Justice Minister Abdol-Ali Lotfi on Mossadegh's arrest;
- Aqideh Morteza Kamal, the municipal chief;
- a Kashani letter to the finance minister;
- an order for implementing the fourth point of a decree; and
- an interview by General Batmanghelidj.

Printed p. 441 resumes inside the Kashani-to-finance-minister item and discusses
the 1933 oil concession and subsequent oil arrangements. This proves that the
gap concerns an immediate-post-coup legal, administrative, and oil-policy
document cluster. It does **not** license reconstruction of the missing text.

For present campaign work, use the official and contemporary record families
(`SUP-006`, `SUP-007`, `SUP-009`, `SUP-010`, `SUP-022`, and `SUP-062`–`SUP-069`)
for any claim those documents can independently establish. Keep the exact
sixteen pages on the acquisition queue only for quotation, provenance
comparison, or Makki's editorial sequence.

## Citation rule

Writers should cite the source that actually supplies the claim. A claim
supported by Bazargan, a newspaper issue, a Majles scan, or an official
document must not be labeled as if it came from inaccessible Makki or Dehnavi.
Where the substitute cannot establish exact wording, private intent, a payment
chain, or documentary completeness, preserve that uncertainty.
