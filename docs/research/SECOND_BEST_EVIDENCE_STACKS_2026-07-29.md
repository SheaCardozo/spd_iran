# Second-best evidence stacks for inaccessible decision records

**Reviewed:** 2026-07-29
**Scope:** Iranian cabinet and Prime Ministry records; First Senate session
192; Mohammad Nasser Qashqai's *Salhā-ye bohrān*

## Result

None of the three desired originals was found in a complete, inspectable online
form. They therefore remain acquisition targets, but they are not equally broad
implementation blockers. Existing online and local evidence can support
carefully bounded scenes at the level of public decisions, institutional
behavior, attributed testimony, and reconstructed political relationships.
The missing originals become blocking only when a claim requires private
ministerial deliberation, named senatorial conduct, or the diary's
date-specific wording and chronology.

The substitution rule is:

1. establish the formal outcome from an official act or proceeding;
2. add contemporary reporting for timing, reaction, and attributed conduct;
3. use participant testimony for remembered motives and relationships;
4. test the reconstruction against specialist scholarship; and
5. do not invent private debate, attendance, individual votes, quotations, or
   certainty that the surviving evidence does not provide.

## 1. Iranian cabinet and Prime Ministry records

| Question | Best accessible evidence | What it supports | What it does not support |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| What was formally decided? | Certified Council of Ministers records in [`SUP-066`](AVAILABLE_SOURCES.md#sup-066), authenticated by [`SUP-067`](AVAILABLE_SOURCES.md#sup-067), with the ICJ oral context in [`SUP-062`](AVAILABLE_SOURCES.md#sup-062) | The two decisions of 23 July 1951, their formal language, and their documentary status | A continuous minute book, full attendance, or the preceding discussion |
| How did proposals reach cabinet? | Institutional submission rules in [`SUP-068`](AVAILABLE_SOURCES.md#sup-068), a numbered Finance-to-cabinet proposal chain in [`SUP-069`](AVAILABLE_SOURCES.md#sup-069), NLAI oil-negotiation reports in [`SUP-063`](AVAILABLE_SOURCES.md#sup-063), and public legal outputs | A traceable proposal–approval–implementation chain for selected measures | An assumption that every proposal followed the same path or was fully debated |
| What did ministers say publicly? | Majles proceedings and confidence debates in [`SUP-007`](AVAILABLE_SOURCES.md#sup-007), decrees and enacted texts in [`SUP-006`](AVAILABLE_SOURCES.md#sup-006), and Mossadegh correspondence in [`SUP-064`](AVAILABLE_SOURCES.md#sup-064) | Cabinet composition, public defenses, confidence, statutory authority, and selected implementation instructions | Private reservations or an undocumented cabinet consensus |
| Is any internal disagreement visible? | [`SUP-010`](AVAILABLE_SOURCES.md#sup-010), FRUS second edition, document 78, local PDF p. 287 / printed p. 250 | A U.S. cable reporting Hossein Ala's account that the Finance Minister warned the 10 June 1952 cabinet meeting of a “state of danger” and declined responsibility for the consequences | The Iranian minute, a verbatim transcript, the full debate, attendance, or the response of each minister |
| How were appointments and relationships remembered? | Selected Harvard oral histories in [`SUP-061`](AVAILABLE_SOURCES.md#sup-061) and participant memoirs in [`SUP-071`](AVAILABLE_SOURCES.md#sup-071)–[`SUP-082`](AVAILABLE_SOURCES.md#sup-082) | Leads and attributed testimony about access, appointments, dismissal, factional relationships, and implementation | Neutral or contemporaneous proof of motives |
| What did foreign interlocutors report? | Official U.S. records in `SUP-010` and the Turkish diplomatic reconstruction in [`SUP-085`](AVAILABLE_SOURCES.md#sup-085), especially printed p. 59 | What foreign officials reported, whom they met, and how they interpreted particular measures | Objective proof of Iranian motives or a replacement for Iranian institutional records |

### Implementation boundary

A cabinet or adviser scene may use the surviving stack when it distinguishes:

- the formal act;
- the publicly stated position;
- a contemporary attributed report;
- a later participant's recollection; and
- the project's cautious inference.

It may not assign a minister a private proposal, veto, delay, exact speech, or
vote solely because the eventual ministry or cabinet adopted a measure. A
continuous archive inventory or selected underlying file remains necessary for
that level of personalization.

## 2. First Senate proceedings, session 192

Session 192 met on 20 Mordad 1331 / 11 August 1952. No official scan of the
complete proceeding was found.

| Question | Best accessible evidence | Evidentiary limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What passed, and when? | The enacted law and Senate colophon in [`SUP-006`](AVAILABLE_SOURCES.md#sup-006) | Establishes the legal result, not the debate |
| How did the measure return to the Majles? | The Senate letter, debate, and standing vote printed in [`SUP-007`](AVAILABLE_SOURCES.md#sup-007) on 21 Mordad | Establishes lower-house handling, not the preceding Senate speeches |
| What was the aggregate Senate division? | A contemporary *Le Monde* dispatch of 12 August 1952, attributed to AFP/UP/AP, reports 26–4 | Provisional aggregate only; it does not name voters or reproduce the roll |
| What was the wider institutional setting? | Official roster [`SUP-053`](AVAILABLE_SOURCES.md#sup-053), enacted texts, chamber rules, and specialist parliamentary history | Supports chamber composition and procedure in general, not attendance at session 192 |
| How did contemporaries react? | [`SUP-085`](AVAILABLE_SOURCES.md#sup-085), printed p. 59, records Turkish ambassador Ali Türkgeldi and Ahmad Matin-Daftari opposing the powers law after Senate passage | External reaction, not evidence of what senators said inside the sitting |

### Implementation boundary

The existing stack supports a Senate-level approval or obstruction mechanic,
the date and enacted result, and a carefully attributed 26–4 aggregate. It does
not support named votes, exact attendance, attributed Senate speeches, or a
member-by-member amendment sequence. Session 192 is therefore a targeted
enhancement for a detailed chamber scene, not a blocker for representing the
Senate at institutional scale.

The public legal transcription and the Senate's internal rules are useful
discovery carriers, but should be checked against official print where exact
wording matters:

- <https://nezamat.ir/post-1077/>
- <https://nezamat.ir/post-19569/>

The contemporary aggregate report is:

- <https://www.lemonde.fr/archives/article/1952/08/12/le-senat-vote-les-pleins-pouvoirs-a-m-mossadegh-qui-renonce-a-proroger-la-loi-martiale_1999143_1819218.html>

## 3. Mohammad Nasser Qashqai, *Salhā-ye bohrān*

No complete online copy was found after title, edition, repository, and reader
searches. The first-edition catalog record remains
[NCID `BA67968060`](https://ci.nii.ac.jp/ncid/BA67968060); the third edition is
`BB10783459`.

The strongest accessible substitute is the seven-tape official Harvard oral
history preserved under [`SUP-061`](AVAILABLE_SOURCES.md#sup-061), used with:

- the third-party searchable Persian transcript at
  <https://iranhistory.net/ghashghaei0/> as a navigation aid only;
- Lois Beck's [`MAJ-S19`](AVAILABLE_SOURCES.md#maj-s19) for Qashqai political
  hierarchy, National Front alignment, and post-coup punishment;
- parliamentary proceedings, contemporary diplomatic reporting, and the main
  coup histories for public acts and outside observations.

The third-party page has no stated transcriber or editorial method and divides
the interview under eight “tape” headings, whereas Harvard exposes seven
cataloged audio objects. An unchanged local HTML copy and metadata note are
preserved inside the ignored `SUP-061` collection. Search hits must be
time-aligned and verified against the official audio before quotation or
claim-level use.

### Implementation boundary

The stack can support cautiously attributed Qashqai relationships, political
orientation, organization, and remembered choices when checked against
contemporary evidence. It cannot reproduce the diary's day-by-day chronology,
edition-specific language, or a dated private assertion that appears nowhere
else. *Salhā-ye bohrān* remains a desirable targeted acquisition for those
claims, not a general blocker for modeling the Qashqai network.

## Acquisition triggers

Return to acquisition rather than extending inference when a proposed scene
requires any of the following:

- a particular minister's private proposal, resistance, delay, or exact
  implementation instruction;
- a named senator's attendance, speech, amendment, or vote in session 192; or
- a quotation, date-specific private observation, or day-by-day sequence found
  only in *Salhā-ye bohrān*.
