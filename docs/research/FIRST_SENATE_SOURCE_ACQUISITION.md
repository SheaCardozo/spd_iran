# First-Senate proceedings acquisition record

- **Audit date:** 2026-07-26
- **Target:** *Muzākerāt-e Majles-e Senā* (مذاکرات مجلس سنا), First
  Senate, 20 Bahman 1328–28 Aban 1332 / 9 February 1950–19 November 1953
- **Current result:** The official retrospective membership roster is local as
  [`SUP-053`](AVAILABLE_SOURCES.md#sup-053). Exact proceeding records are
  identified, but no session scan is locally accessible.
- **Implementation consequence:** Chamber-level outcomes and broad procedure
  can be reconstructed from official laws, Majles records, contemporary
  reporting, FRUS, and scholarship. Named divisions, attendance, exact
  speeches, amendments, and reconstructed dialogue remain prohibited.

The Senate's printed proceedings are controlling evidence of what the chamber
recorded as said, moved, and decided. They do not establish that every factual
assertion made by a senator was true.

## What is already established

- The official enacted-law colophon and the Senate letter read to the
  Seventeenth Majles on 21 Mordad independently establish Senate passage of
  the six-month powers bill on 20 Mordad. The session-192 catalog date is not
  the sole evidence for that outcome.
- Seventeenth-Majles session 23, 12 Mordad 1331, printed p. 13 (local
  `SUP-007` session PDF p. 7), records the lower-house standing division as
  “the majority rose” and “passed,” without a number.
- A contemporary *Le Monde* report from Tehran, published 12 August 1952 and
  attributed to AFP, United Press, and AP, reports a Senate result of **26–4**
  after an adjournment and a ten-member delegation sought clarification.
  This is strong contemporary corroboration, not an official roll call.
- Sessions 190–193 establish that the Senate sat on 13, 18, and 20 Mordad and
  then not again until 26 Shahrivar. A claimed First-Senate sitting on
  30 Mordad is chronologically wrong.

Do not infer total attendance, abstentions, or individual votes from the
provisional 26–4 count.

## Confirmed carriers and request routes

1. **SDIL:** its [digital-library registration
   form](https://library.sdil.ac.ir/digital-library/) advertises download
   allowances but requires Iranian identity and contact information. Do not
   submit another person's identity or payment details. A direct staff request
   should name the session, Persian date, and GUID.
2. **University of Chicago:** [*Muzakirat-i Majlis-i
   Sina*](https://www.lib.uchicago.edu/e/collections/mideast/medoc-cat/persian-oprm.html),
   `microfm K13.U84`, contains sessions 38–126 (15 Mehr 1329–13 Mehr 1330).
   The University of Washington's *Middle East in Microform* union list
   independently confirms the range.
3. **National Diet Library:** catalogs the print serial under `Y771-M5` /
   `a1000121607`; it is not an inspected digital copy.
4. **Royal Official Gazette route:** Azimi, *Iran: The Crisis of Democracy*,
   bibliography p. 406 (local PDF p. 422), identifies the Senate debates as
   part of *Ruznamih-yi Rasmi-yi Kishvar-i Shahanshahi* for 1950–52.

## Negative and rejected results

- Mashruteh's indexed Senate transcript set concerns the seventh term, not the
  First Senate.
- Iran Archive and Internet Archive searches produced no usable First-Senate
  proceeding or Royal Official Gazette run.
- Wikimedia Commons contains a First-Senate membership booklet but no
  identified First-Senate debate run.
- A Commons file generically titled “session 190” was inspected and rejected:
  its title page identifies Second-Senate session 190, 28 Mehr 1335.
- An Iran Archive appendix reproduces an official law colophon already held in
  `SUP-006`; it does not supply the proceeding.

## Current use gate

Safe now:

- chamber membership and appointed/elected origin from `SUP-053`;
- enacted outcomes and dates from official law and Majles records;
- broad constitutional conflict from the specialist histories;
- the provisional 26–4 aggregate with explicit wire-service provenance.

Still requires the proceeding:

- named votes or abstentions;
- exact attendance and quorum;
- speeches, amendments, procedural objections, or quotations;
- a member-by-member Senate alignment reconstructed from a single division.
